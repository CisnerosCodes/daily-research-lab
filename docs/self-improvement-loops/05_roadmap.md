# Roadmap: where the programme goes after the 2026-08-13 update

This synthesizes the four companion documents — the lessons review (`01`), the self-improvement-loops survey (`02`), the prior-art audit (`03`), and the repo review (`04`) — plus the archive-conditioning experiment (`experiments/2026-08-13_archive-conditioning-rate-vs-asymptote/`). It replaces scattered ambition with one thesis and a sequenced plan.

## The one-sentence conclusion of the whole update

**The programme owns exactly one defensible, head-turning contribution — asymptotic richness estimation as the evaluation frame for diversity interventions and self-improvement loops — and everything else it has built is either evidence for that contribution or territory the literature already claimed.**

Two independent research agents converged on this from opposite directions. The prior-art audit found that of the programme's seven claims, four (species-richness estimation, model-union, taxonomy seeding, archive conditioning) reduce to the rate-versus-asymptote frame, one (assumption negation) is a narrow operator refinement, and two (archive conditioning as a mechanism, Ratchet's dual-loop architecture) are dead — claimed by NoveltyBench/Denial Prompting/Nova and by EvoX/SeaEvo/EvoPH/ShinkaEvolve respectively. The survey, surveying the loop literature instead of the diversity literature, concluded that "the generator's functional richness and the verifier's information content are the two independent inputs to a self-improvement loop, and every documented plateau is one of them hitting zero marginal return... no published work measures both in the same run" — and ranked "richness-estimator instrumentation of evolutionary archives" as its number one wedge.

One reconciliation the two reports force: the survey searched for Chao1-on-LLM-outputs and found nothing; the audit found **KnowSum (arXiv 2506.02058)**, which applies smoothed Good-Turing unseen-species estimation to LLM outputs with "measuring output diversity" as a named application, and **UCS (arXiv 2604.12015)** from the same group. The audit's positive find governs. The statistical machinery is claimed; what remains unclaimed is using the estimated asymptote as the **classifier for interventions** (walks-faster versus raises-ceiling) and as the **instrument inside a running loop**. KnowSum must be cited in the first paragraph of anything the programme publishes, not discovered by a reviewer.

## Why this thesis connects the two halves of the programme

The E-series measured that a single generator's idea support has a finite estimated richness, that prompt-level interventions do not enlarge it, and that component swaps (models, knowledge, framings) do. Ratchet is a FunSearch-descendant loop whose plateau, per the survey's synthesis, is predicted to be a diversity event: AlphaEvolve names stagnation-without-diversity-mechanisms as its own limitation, ShinkaEvolve devotes two of its three innovations to diversity machinery, LEVI demonstrates that diversity-poor archives force compensation with more expensive models (3.3-6.7x cost), and EvoDiverse (ICML 2026) names selection-pressure-driven diversity collapse "a critical bottleneck." The field says "diversity matters" in prose; the E-series built the estimator that turns it into a measured, predictive quantity. The bridge experiment is: instrument an evolutionary loop with the richness estimator, show functional richness saturates before best-score saturates, and show that the richness-saturation point predicts the score plateau. Then swap the generator components the E-series validated (model union, knowledge injection, framing union) and show the loop's ceiling moves. That is a paper both the diversity literature and the loops literature would have to cite.

## The sequenced plan

### Now (this update)
1. **Archive-conditioning rate-versus-asymptote experiment** — running. Whatever the outcome, it is the first application of the classifier: if archive feedback raises the rate but not the Chao1 asymptote, the classifier has its first sorted intervention and the default diversity move of agentic loops is demoted to a sampling-efficiency trick; if it moves the asymptote, the E-series "prompt axis is exhausted" claim gains a carve-out and the classifier caught it. Judge noise is measured for the first time in the programme.

### Next 2-4 weeks (API-only, laptop-scale)
2. **Wedge 1 — richness instrumentation on EvoTrace.** arXiv 2605.20086 released 121 complete evolutionary code-search runs (EvoTrace) plus a replay tool. Apply the richness estimators (Chao1 as an explicit lower bound, plus ACE, jackknife, and a coverage-based estimator, with clustering-threshold sensitivity) to functional-class counts across the traces. Test: does richness saturation lead score saturation? Decompose generation-side collapse (falling rate of newly *proposed* classes) from archive-side collapse (falling rate of *retained* classes at flat proposal rate) — a decomposition the survey believes is unpublished. **Precondition to verify first: whether EvoTrace includes rejected candidates.** Possibly zero API spend.
3. **Estimator-robustness pass on existing E-series data.** Re-estimate the ~54 ceiling with Chao-Bunge, jackknife-2, and Hill-number extrapolation, and report the spread. Both agents flagged Chao1's assumptions (closed population, homogeneous detection) as the reviewer's strongest attack; the defensible claim is "a lower bound under a stated clustering procedure with sensitivity analysis," and that reframe costs one afternoon.
4. **Verbalized Sampling asymptote test.** The survey identified the sharpest falsifiable exposure of the programme's central claim: Verbalized Sampling (arXiv 2510.01171) is a prompt-level intervention with a measured 1.6-2.1x diversity gain. If it raises the estimated asymptote rather than only the rate, "prompt engineering cannot pass the ceiling" is dead, and the programme should want to know before a reviewer does. Run it through the same rate-versus-asymptote harness as the archive-conditioning experiment. The precise, defensible form of the claim to test: *decoding-level and instruction-level interventions raise sampling efficiency within a fixed support; only interventions that change the conditioning distribution (model, knowledge, framing) extend the support.*

### The Kaggle/GPU phase (already briefed, now re-prioritized)
5. **E3/E5 from the Kaggle handoff proceed as written**, with one addition from this update: every arm reports estimated asymptotes with judge-noise bars, not observed distinct counts alone, and the model-union arm at fixed 7-8B size across three open-weight families becomes the clean test of claim 4's surviving delta (union richness versus single-model *asymptote* — no published ensemble-diversity paper compares to the asymptote). The contrary literature the writeup must engage: "We're Different, We're the Same" (arXiv 2501.19361) and "Human diversity fuels collective creativity" (arXiv 2607.26899), both of which dispute low cross-model overlap on different units of analysis.

### Wedge 2 after the instrument is proven
6. **Decompose the heterogeneity axes at matched budget.** DEI (arXiv 2605.27130, ICML 2026 workshop) already showed heterogeneous model families beat homogeneous ensembles at matched budget in a QD loop — so the E9 headline is partially claimed in loop form. What nobody has done is the factorial: model family versus knowledge conditioning versus framing/persona, at fixed token budget, measured on estimated richness with a mechanical verifier. This answers the builder's question — given a fixed budget, buy more models, more knowledge, or more framings? — and directly generalizes E9 into the loop setting. Cite DEI as premise, not competitor.

### Ratchet's future
7. **Ratchet is a demo, not a contribution — repurpose it as the instrumented testbed.** The dual-loop architecture is claimed (EvoX by name, SeaEvo, EvoPH). Its honest role now: a cheap, transparent loop to carry the richness instrument. Concrete upgrades, in order of expected yield per the survey's evidence: (a) log per-generation functional-class richness of proposals — the instrument itself; (b) proposer-model union via a bandit (ShinkaEvolve's mechanism — cite it); (c) MAP-Elites-style or novelty-rejection archive in place of top-8 truncation; (d) advance the champion on train but log held-out at every crowning; (e) drop the guidance-block bandit or power it properly — with single-digit uses per block it selects on noise, which is the E8 stacking failure re-enacted (and SeaEvo now owns strategy-level statistics anyway).

### Deferred, explicitly
- **Wedge 3 (verifier ladder x observability)** — the most citable single design in the survey, but it needs a task with a hidden held-out oracle and real replicate budget; schedule after Wedges 1-2 produce the instrument and the habit.
- **Assumption negation** — arm C of the running experiment is its trial. If it does not open arm-unique territory there, drop it; IDEAFix (arXiv 2606.00875) already reports homogenization persisting across 25 defixation strategies, and the burden of proof is on the operator.
- **Dyadic accountability / logistics-piggyback pilots from E9** — real-world validation is outside this lab's remit; the falsification-survey design is written and can be handed to a health-services collaborator as-is.

## Lab-rule changes adopted from this update

From the repo review, applied lab-wide: (1) an arc may only open with a stated stopping number; (2) the index/registry headline must not claim more than the README caveats permit; (3) the ten-times-budget control is mandatory before any fitting-speed claim graduates to a quality claim; (4) named-instrument calibration debts (backlog item 51) block further experiments on that instrument. From the lessons review, applied to all ideation work: five replicates minimum, judge noise measured like seed noise, escape metrics over diversity metrics, coherence gates on every arm, novelty check before code with the verdict recorded. From the audit, one meta-rule: **before claiming any mechanism, search 2026 arXiv specifically** — five of seven claims were claimed, four of them by papers from the last eight months.

## What "turns heads" actually looks like from here

The audit said it plainly: this is one paper, not four. Its working title is the thesis — *Rate or Ceiling? Classifying Diversity Interventions and Self-Improvement Plateaus by Estimated Support Richness* — with the E-series as motivating measurements, the archive-conditioning experiment as the first sorted intervention, EvoTrace reanalysis as the loop-side demonstration, and the Kaggle model-union replication as the decomposition. Every mechanism in it is cited to its owner; the contribution is the instrument and what it reveals. That is the shape of work a big lab stops scrolling for: not a new loop, but the measurement that explains why all the existing loops plateau — and a measured rule for which component swap moves the ceiling.

## Update, 2026-08-27: the classifier's first sorted mechanism is back in question

The budget-extension run (`experiments/2026-08-27_archive-conditioning-budget-extension/`) was the test the powered replication committed to, and it did not go the way the thesis above assumes. Under the pre-registered rule, "archive conditioning walks a fixed support faster" survives — the crossing margin against the control arm's maximum estimator is +2.67 classes at 0.48x the paired SD. But arm B's mean observed count at 96 ideas (33.4) is above **all three** of the control arm's mean ceiling estimates (Chao1-bc 27.0, ACE 28.3, jackknife-2 30.5), it crossed in 6 of 8 replicates, the B−A gap ended at its 16-round maximum with no turnover, and the self-audit prediction that the estimators would climb with budget was **wrong** — they were flat, which makes a still-climbing observed count passing a stable ceiling estimate harder to explain as walks-faster.

Three consequences for the plan above.

1. **Section "What turns heads" overstates the current state.** The working title *Rate or Ceiling?* is still the right frame, but the paper cannot present archive conditioning as the classifier's cleanly sorted example until the estimator-free test below is done. The classifier's value does not depend on which way that test lands; the specific sorting claim does.
2. **The estimator-robustness pass (item 3) is promoted from an afternoon of tidying to a load-bearing prerequisite.** The defence that keeps walks-faster alive is that Chao1, ACE, and jackknife-2 are lower bounds that can be *stably* biased low. That is a claim about the instrument, and the instrument is the contribution, so it has to be characterized rather than asserted.
3. **A new item goes to the front of the queue: the single-arm catch-up test.** Run the iid control arm alone to roughly 384 ideas and ask whether its observed distinct-class count ever reaches the 33.4 that archive conditioning reached at 96. No estimator sits in that comparison. If the control saturates below B's 96-idea count, walks-faster is dead on observed data and the E-series "prompt axis is exhausted" conclusion takes the carve-out its own falsification clause anticipated. If the control climbs past, the estimators were biased low at these budgets, walks-faster is vindicated on the strongest evidence available, and the lab learns that its instrument understates ceilings — which is itself a finding the paper needs.

Also recorded from that run, independent of the above: the rate effect is now measured at three budgets and grows monotonically (+2.4 per 24 ideas, +5.3 per 48, +8.75 per 96), so the practical rule for loop builders stands regardless of which mechanism explains it; the incoherence tax replicates without accelerating; and judge noise rose to a between-judge SD of 3.0 classes on the conditioned arm with mean pairwise ARI down to 0.789, the lab's worst recorded, plausibly because the pooled judging call grew to 192 items. Pool size is now a parameter to control, not a free choice.
